# Why people stop scrolling — and the Buzzend content rules

You don't need a psychology degree. You need to know the 12 levers below,
and the rule each one gives us. Every video we make should pull at least
2–3 of these levers **in the first 3 seconds**.

## The levers

**1 · Attention is a pattern-break.**
Feeds are endless sameness; the brain stops for the unexpected. A phone
on the floor counting a human's squats out loud *is* unexpected.
→ **Rule: the counter must be visible and moving within the first 2
seconds of every video.** Never open with a logo, a greeting, or "hey
guys".

**2 · Curiosity — an open question the brain must close.**
"Can AI count my push-ups?" forces the viewer to wait for the answer.
→ **Rule: question hooks must be answered ON CAMERA within ~3 seconds**
(the counter ticking up = the answer). Never bait without paying off.

**3 · Problem recognition — "that's me."**
People stop for their own problem said plainly: losing count, restarting
every Monday, hating the gym, working out alone.
→ **Rule: name the problem in the viewer's words** (we collect exact
phrases from comments in the hooks-inbox), not in marketing words.

**4 · Relatability beats polish.**
A real person in a normal living room outperforms a studio ad. Slightly
imperfect = trustworthy.
→ **Rule: film in real homes, normal clothes, normal bodies. Never make
it look like an ad.** Beginner energy is our advantage, not a weakness.

**5 · Emotion — surprise and delight, not hype.**
The honest reaction "wait, it actually counted that?" is our emotional
core.
→ **Rule: capture genuine reactions. Never script fake excitement.**

**6 · Social proof — people trust people.**
Comments, duets, "my friend challenged me", visible member counts.
→ **Rule: screenshot great comments (with permission) and turn them into
content. Reply to everything — replies are public proof someone's home.**

**7 · Authority without arrogance.**
We earn trust by demonstrating, not claiming. Showing the counter work,
including when it misses a sloppy rep, is more convincing than
"most accurate AI".
→ **Rule: show, never claim. A missed rep on a bad-form squat is GOOD
content ("why didn't this rep count?").**

**8 · Transformation — before → after.**
Fitness runs on visible change: day 1 vs day 7 of a streak, 5 shaky
push-ups → 20 clean ones.
→ **Rule: build series content (streak diaries, "30 days of…") so
transformation accumulates on the profile.**

**9 · FOMO — used gently.**
"245 people joined this challenge" works. Fake urgency ("last chance!")
destroys trust for an app that's always available.
→ **Rule: only real numbers, only real challenges. No countdown-timer
theatrics.**

**10 · Novelty + specificity.**
"AI fitness app" is vague noise. "My phone counted 47 squats" is
specific and new.
→ **Rule: always use a real number in the hook when we can.** "47 squats"
beats "my workout". Specific numbers read as true.

**11 · Storytelling — even in 20 seconds.**
Setup (phone goes down) → tension (will it count?) → payoff (it counts)
→ resolution (result screen). That's a complete story.
→ **Rule: every video has the four beats. If a cut has no payoff moment,
it's not done.**

**12 · Community — belonging keeps people.**
People stay for people. Challenges-with-friends is literally our product.
→ **Rule: end challenge content with "Challenge a friend." and treat the
comment section as the product demo of our community.**

## CTA psychology (why we say "Try your first AI workout")

- A CTA works when it's **one** small, concrete next step. Two CTAs = zero
  CTAs. Never combine "follow AND download AND comment".
- "Try your first AI workout" beats "Download Buzzend" because it sells
  the *experience*, is low-commitment ("try", "first"), and matches our
  definition of success (a completed workout, not a download).
- Say it once, at the end, spoken + on-screen + in the caption. On
  challenge content, the CTA is "Challenge a friend." instead.

## The 6-point pre-publish sanity check

Before anything goes live, it must pass all six:
1. Counter (or the product truth) visible in the first 2 seconds?
2. At least 2 levers from this page working in the first 3 seconds?
3. Would a stranger, muted, understand "the phone counts your reps"?
4. Is everything shown real (§40 — no faked counts)?
5. Exactly one CTA?
6. No banned words (see `what-is-buzzend.md`)?
