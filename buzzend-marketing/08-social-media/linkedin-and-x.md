# LinkedIn & X (Twitter) — mostly SKIP (here's exactly why)

We researched both. For a 3-person beginner team marketing a consumer
fitness app to 18–35 home exercisers, **neither earns real effort in
2026.** Spreading thin across five platforms produces weak results on
all five.

## LinkedIn — founder-only, 1 post/week max

- **Why not more:** LinkedIn pays off for B2B and professional
  audiences. Our buyers aren't hiring-managers scrolling LinkedIn for
  workout apps.
- **What it IS good for:** hiring, partnerships, press, app-store
  business contacts.
- **Minimum viable approach (≤1 h/week, founder's personal profile —
  NOT a company page, personal profiles massively out-reach pages):**
  one build-in-public post per week, e.g. *"What we learned shipping an
  on-device AI rep counter"*. If posting video, native vertical video
  currently gets a big distribution boost; PDF carousels also perform.
- Owner: whoever founds/runs Buzzend — NOT one of the 3 marketing lanes.
  The team may repurpose an existing script into a LinkedIn draft, but
  it never displaces a calendar slot.

## X/Twitter — claim and park

- **Why skip:** free accounts get a fraction of Premium reach, ~half of
  small-account posts get zero engagement, and links in the post body
  cut reach 30–50% — hostile conditions for beginners marketing a
  link-dependent app.
- **Do exactly this, once:** claim @buzzend (or closest), set the avatar,
  bio = positioning line + link-in-bio URL, pin one good demo video.
  Done. Revisit only if Buzzend catches on with the tech crowd (the
  "can AI count my reps" angle could someday — a decision for a monthly
  review, not now).

## The rule this file exists to enforce

If anyone suggests "we should also be on [platform]", the answer is:
show which existing platform we've saturated first. Three video
platforms + FB mirror is already a full plate for three people.
